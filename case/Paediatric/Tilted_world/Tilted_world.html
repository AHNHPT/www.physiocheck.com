🧪 Key Points

  • Early physiotherapy intervention has good evidence for congenital muscular torticollis (CMT) — improving cervical ROM, reducing muscle tightness, correcting head posture, and improving functional outcomes (Desai et al. 2024).
  • Before reaching to our torticollis toolbox, red-flag screening is MANDATORY — to confirm the baby really has congenital MUSCULAR torticollis that physio can help with, and not something else needing medical attention.
  • """Bony end-feel vs muscular end-feel""" is a key discriminator: CMT typically restricts rotation and side flexion only, with a firm, springy muscular end-feel — structural causes (e.g. Klippel-Feil syndrome) show a hard, bony restriction across multiple planes.
  • Liam's vacuum-assisted delivery and prolonged second stage of labour are shared risk factors for both birth trauma (Moro reflex asymmetry) and Developmental Dysplasia of the Hip — the same "intrauterine packaging" forces can affect both the neck and the hips.
  • Management should target the actual short/tight structure — for Liam, the LEFT SCM — through positioning, active facilitation of the opposing weak muscles, and manual stretching that is gentle, never forced, and paired with play.
