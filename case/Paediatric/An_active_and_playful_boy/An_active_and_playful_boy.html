Case: An active and playful boy

Johnny, a 3-year-old boy, has been diagnosed with global developmental delay.
He was referred by a paediatrician for out-patient physiotherapy for developmental training.

This is the 1st OPD Physio session, which has been scheduled on 20 December 2025. The weather today is sunny yet with the cold front, the temperature drops to 10 degrees Celsius.

Johnny is brought by his mother with 45 minutes late. He wears a long-sleeved T-shirt and frayed trousers. He looks timid and slender, and is shivering. When asked if there were any concern on Johnny’s gross motor development, his mother replied: “I don’t think Johnny needs physiotherapy - just the doctor wrote referral for us to come here. He is good in everything."

Question 1
Option A - Check blood profile
When encountering bruises in children, we should be alerted.
Johnny had bruises over bilateral thighs and ankle joints. DDx could be accident, NAI (non-accidental injury) or other underlying diseases. Easy bruising in children could be due to platelet disorders that the blood has difficulty in clotting due to shortage of platelets.
Although Johnny does not have any past medial history of bleeding disorders, considering his recent RSV and EV/RV infection, we need to be cautious if he developed immune thrombocytopenia (ITP) after viral infection. ITP of childhood is an acquired immune-mediated, and usually self-limiting, condition of low platelet counts, which could lead to easy bruising in children.

Option B - Check head and neck, torso and mouth
Following above about Johnny’s bruises, platelet disorders have been ruled out. Johnny’s mother said that Johnny was very active and playful at home and loves bouncing around and jumping down from sofa and hence got hurt easily.

However, according to the physiotherapy assessment results, his gross motor performance was around 18 months only, with poor jumping performance. A mismatch was noted between carer’s explanation and the child’s actual developmental performance. NAI was therefore suspected. According to Bruising Clinical Decision Rule for children less than 4 years old, any one of the criteria below was highly suspicious of child abuse: any bruise found in torso, ear or neck; any bruise on infants less than 4 months; any bruise found over frenulums, angle of jaw, cheek, eyelid or sclera; pattered bruises.



(Refernce: Pierce, M. C., Kaczor, K., Lorenz, D. J., Bertocci, G., Fingarson, A. K., Makoroﬀ, K., ... & Leventhal, J. M. (2021). Validation of a clinical decision rule to predict abuse in young children based on bruising characteristics. JAMA network open, 4(4), e215832-e215832.)

Option C - Check single leg stand
Balance is one of the gross motor physiotherapy assessment areas for children. A 3-year-old child should be able to perform a single leg stand for 3 seconds. However, for Johnny’s case, his unexplained multiple new and old patterned bruises might point to suspicious child abuse which are more emergent to be addressed and managed. Therefore, checking his single leg balance is in lower priority at this moment.

Option D - Check vitals
Observation is very important for physiotherapists working with children.
Apart from observing their gait and movement pattern, we need to pay attention to everything in fact - interaction between the child and the carer, the carer’s response, the child’s behaviours, clothings etc. Going back to Johnny’s case, he was shivering in his T shirt in a freezing cold day. Vitals need to be checked if he is suﬀering from hypothermia which needs urgent medical care.

Johnny’s unexplained multiple new and old paeerned bruises might point to suspicious child abuse. Child protection mechanism needs to be activated and need further medical attention and management. Immediate escort to AED is needed if he has unstable vitals and needs urgent medical care (eg respiratory failure which needs respiratory support, loss of consciousness). Otherwise, if his vitals are stable, hospital MCCA (Medical Coordinator of Child Abuse) should be consulted for further management. Direct admission to ward for further management would be arranged if necessary instead of long waiting in AED and prevent secondary trauma to the child from repeated questioning.

Option D - Mandatory reporting
The Mandatory Reporting of Child Abuse Ordinance (Cap. 650) has been eﬀective from January 20, 2026, requiring 25 categories of professionals in education, social welfare and healthcare to report suspected child abuse involving serious harm or risk of harm. Failure to report can result in fines and up to 3 months' imprisonment. Johnny’s unexplained multiple new and old patterned bruises might point to suspicious child abuse, falling to the criteria of mandatory reporting - at real risk of suﬀering serious harm.
