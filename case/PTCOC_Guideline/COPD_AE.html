1.1 Clinical Assessment Steps

For physiotherapists initiating an acute ward consult:

Step 1: History Taking

Analyze context of onset, current admission details, asthma/sinus allergies, previous hospitalizations, lifestyle/exposures, family support, and level of baseline dyspnoea.

Step 2: Physical Examination

Inspection & Palpation

Accessory muscle use, breathing pattern/asynchrony, expansion, chest wall compliance.

Percussion & Auscultation

Identify localized secretions, consolidation, bronchospasm (wheezes), diminished entry.

Step 3: Clinical Analysis

Diagnose the functional deficit profile: Is it dominated by **dyspnoea/hyperinflation**, **exercise limitations**, or **mucus retention / cough weakness**?

GUIDELINE EVIDENCE HIERARCHY (SIGN System)

Evidence Levels:

1++ / 1+: High-quality systematic reviews / RCTs with low risk of bias.

2++ / 2+: Strong cohort/case studies with low bias.

3 / 4: Non-analytic studies / Expert consensus.

Recommendation Grades:

Grade A: Direct level 1++ evidence consistency.

Grade B: Level 1-, 2++ directly applicable.

Grade C: Level 2+ or extrapolated.

Grade D: Level 3/4 or extrapolated 2+.

Note: Recommendation grade reflects structural evidence strength, not clinical importance.

Physiotherapy Management of Dyspnoea for COPD

Primary goals: Reduce regional dynamic hyperinflation, decrease work of breathing, optimize regional ventilation and elevate mechanical breathing efficiency.

Grade A • Level 1++ ✔ RECOMMENDED

Inspiratory Muscle Training (IMT)

Recommended as an adjunct in selected patients showing significant reduction of inspiratory muscle strength and reporting dyspnoea during physical activity. Only effective if using threshold loading.

Clinical Protocol: Intensity load must be set at ≥30% of Maximum Inspiratory Pressure (MIP). Apply in patients unable to tolerate standard exercise.
Grade C • Level 2- ✔ SELECTIVE

Pursed-Lips Breathing (PLB)

Reduces breathing frequency, dynamic airway collapse, and dyspnoea. It optimizes tidal volume and increases oxygen saturation during activities of daily living (ADLs).

Clinical Protocol: Test individual response first. Teach patients to integrate PLB dynamically during activities that precipitate acute breathlessness.
ALERT / WARNING
Grade B • Level 1- ✘ NOT RECOMMENDED

Diaphragmatic Breathing

DO NOT use for moderate-to-severe COPD! It increases the overall work and oxygen cost of breathing, enhances chest wall asynchrony, and reduces mechanical efficiency.

Safety Rule: Terminate immediately if patient exhibits abdominal paradoxical breathing patterns, chest fatigue, or worsening dyspnoea.
Grade C • Level 2- ✔ RECOMMENDED

Forward Lean Position

Significantly decreases the sensation of dyspnoea. Forward leaning with arm support allows auxiliary chest muscles (pectoralis major/minor) to elevate the ribcage effectively and support ventilation.

Clinical Protocol: Highly useful during rollator-assisted ambulation. Instruct patients to rest forearms on the rollator platform to offload breathing work.
Grade B • Level 1- ⚡ ADJUNCT

AcuTENS & Acupressure

May be considered as an additional short-term option to improve lung function indices ($FEV_1$) and temporarily reduce anxiety and dyspnoea.

Standard Protocols:

• AcuTENS: Bilateral TENS over EX-B1 (Ding Chuan) acupoint for 45 mins.

• Acupressure: Apply pressure over 5-7 selected points for 1-3 mins.

Grade B • Level 1- ✔ OPTIONAL

Relaxation Exercises

Has beneficial short-term effects on breathlessness by down-regulating sympathetic tone, lower heart rate, and relieving acute anxiety peaks associated with sudden onset dyspnoea.

Clinical Protocol: Target patients presenting with high state anxiety scores during exacerbations. Combine with breathing mechanics training.

Grade C • Level 2- ✔ USEFUL ADJUNCT

Postural Drainage (PD)

Utilizes gravity to facilitate secretion transport. Not supported as a standalone therapy, but highly effective when paired with active breathing cycle maneuvers.

Indications: Consistently add when FET or PEP methods are individually insufficient, and localized segment mucus retention is clinical verified.
Grade B • Level 2++ ✘ NOT EFFECTIVE ROUTINE

Manual Percussion & Vibration

Rigorous studies demonstrate that manual chest percussion provides no additional statistical benefit to standard postural drainage and coughing in acute exacerbation of COPD.

Clinical Lesson: Avoid using routine manual percussion in AECOPD unless explicit pediatric/cystic fibrosis overlay is indicated.
Grade C • Level 2- ⚠️ EXTREME CAUTION

Chest & Abdominal Compression

Manual compression during huffs/coughs decreases peak cough flow (PCF) in standard COPD due to premature dynamic airway compression!

Indications: Only consider in patients with documented extreme neuromuscular respiratory or expiratory muscle weakness.
Grade C • Level 2- ✔ RECOMMENDED

Forced Expiratory Techniques (FET/ACBT)

Utilizes huffing at mid-to-low lung volumes. Active Cycle of Breathing Techniques (ACBT) effectively raises sputum expectorated volume, lowers viscosity, and relieves acute dyspnoea.

Clinical Protocol: Combine cycles of breathing control, thoracic expansion exercises, and huffing to optimize clearance with minimal energy cost.
Grade B • Level 2++ ✔ HIGHLY RECOMMENDED

Positive Expiratory Pressure (PEP)

Increases functional residual capacity (FRC), decreases collateral airway resistance, prevents early airway closure, and relocates deep secretions proximally.

Indications: Highly recommended as a primary active therapy device for patients experiencing excessive mucus production.
Grade C • Level 2- ✔ CONSIDER ADJUNCT

Oscillating PEP (OPEP)

Includes devices like Flutter or Acapella. They generate intra-airway vibration paired with positive pressure to shear sputum from bronchial walls.

Guideline verdict: Evidence lacks extensive long-term RCT support, but can be incorporated into customized home plans for airway clearance.

Acute Phase Triage Timeline

Phase A: Acute Exacerbation Peak

Rule: Exercise and physical activities are CONTRAINDICATED / NOT RECOMMENDED during active, uncompensated respiratory failure.

Phase B: Resolving / Bed-Bound

Severe ventilatory limitations may preclude regular ambulation. Focus on preserving mass. Implement Neuromuscular Electrical Stimulation (NMES) early to quadriceps.

Phase C: Sub-Acute & Ambulatory

Initiate walking or weight-bearing training within 1 week of admission if stable. Introduce quadriceps and lower limb resistance training to combat steroid-induced muscle atrophy.

⚡ Supplemental O2 Strategy during Exercise

1. Rest Hypoxemia / On LTOT

Require O2 supplementation exceeding resting flow levels to completely prevent exercise desaturation (Grade C).

2. Normal Rest but Desaturate on Exertion

Provide supplemental oxygen during exercise training. Relieves dyspnoea/fatigue and maintains target saturation ≥90% (Grade C/B).

3. No Desaturation during Exercise

Routine supplemental oxygen is NOT recommended (Grade C). Offers no training benefit.

Grade B • Level 2++ RECOMMENDED

Lower Extremity Strengthening

Skeletal muscle wasting (especially quadriceps) accelerates during and after acute exacerbations, worsened by high-dose corticosteroids. Resistance training is highly safe, counteracts dysfunction, and does not raise systemic inflammation.

Protocol: Initiate quadriceps loading as soon as acute exacerbation signs begin resolving to halt muscle atrophy.
Grade C • Level 2+ RECOMMENDED

Use of Walking Aids (Rollator)

Walking with a rollator increases self-paced walking distance and time in patients with moderate-to-severe COPD. The built-in arm support forces a "forward-lean" posture, improving diaphragmatic mechanical leverage.

Clinical Impact: Demonstrates direct reduction of dyspnoea and increases functional capacity without negative impacts on arterial oxygen levels.
Grade C • Level 2+ RECOMMENDED

NMES for Deconditioned Patients

Recommended for patients with severely impaired muscle strength or those who are bed-bound during AECOPD and unable to tolerate normal physical exertion.

Clinical Impact: Markedly improves quadriceps strength, limits bedrest-induced disuse atrophy, reduces dyspnoea during minor subsequent tasks, and boosts walking distance.
Grade C • Level 2++ RECOMMENDED

Disease Self-Management Education

Interactive self-management programs are robustly associated with improved health-related quality of life and reduced hospital readmissions. Conversely, *didactical lectures* have minimal impact.

Core Content Checklist:

• Proper inhaler delivery & medication compliance

• Dyspnoea & stress conservation techniques

• Action planning: When to seek immediate emergency help
