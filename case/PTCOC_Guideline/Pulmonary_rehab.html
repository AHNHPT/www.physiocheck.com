Chapter 01

Why pulmonary rehab, and how sure are we?

PR is defined as a comprehensive, patient-tailored intervention — exercise training, education and behaviour change — aimed at improving physical and psychological status and supporting long-term healthy behaviour in people with chronic respiratory disease (ATS, 2023).

A

Dyspnoea, health status, exercise tolerance

Consistently improved in clinically stable patients.

B

Fewer hospitalisations

Reduced re-admission when started ≤4 weeks after an exacerbation.

A

Less anxiety & depression

Meaningful reduction in psychological symptom burden.

B

Self-management + clinician contact

Lowers hospitalisations and ED visits.

A

Broad indication

Indicated for anyone with relevant symptoms or high exacerbation risk — not COPD only.

A

Activity = survival signal

Physical activity level is a strong predictor of mortality.

Reading the evidence grades used throughout this guideline

A

Rich, consistent RCT evidence — ≥2 quality trials or one large unbiased RCT.

B

RCTs with real limitations, or a limited body of evidence overall.

C

Non-randomised trials or observational studies only.

D

Panel consensus — useful guidance, but literature is insufficient.

Chapter 02

Who gets referred, and when

Referral criteria and timing are ultimately set by local policy and physician judgement — the points below are the guideline's reference framework.

Inclusion — in descending priority

1

COPD with a single AECOPD hospitalisation within the past year.

2

COPD with ≥2 AECOPD hospitalisations within the past year.

3

Symptomatic chronic chest patients (incl. COPD), mMRC ≥2.

4

Milder disease — refer with a prevention / adherence-focused programme.

Comorbidities alone should never auto-exclude a referral — review individually with the multidisciplinary PR team. Key referral moments: at diagnosis, at discharge after an exacerbation, or when symptoms are visibly worsening. PR teams should also be equipped to support pre-habilitation before lung cancer or transplant surgery.

✕

Cardiac / non-resp disease

e.g. dementia, severe arthritis or back pain preventing participation.

✕

Poor rehab potential

End-stage disease, e.g. bedbound patients.

✕

Poor motivation

Engagement is essential to benefit from PRP.

✕

Completed PRP <1 yr ago

Consider a refresher programme instead.

If a patient refuses PRP, has mMRC ≤2 and is not on LTOT → refer to HA community support / NAHC for self-management and exercise education instead.

Timing after an acute exacerbation — how the bodies compare

In-hospital PR during an acute exacerbation is also supported as safe and effective — it improves function and strength without prolonging length of stay, and prioritising endurance training pre-discharge shows the most favourable readmission/exercise-capacity outcomes.

Chapter 05

Physiotherapy management — the engine room

Assessment feeds a tailored blend of dyspnoea management, airway clearance, exercise training, and — for patients who can't tolerate conventional training — a set of enhancement strategies.

History to take

Present illness & meds/labs · past respiratory disease, comorbidities, IMV/NIV/HFOT history · hospitalisation history · socioeconomic status, smoking, carer support · home environment & accessibility · premorbid mobility & exercise habit · home LTOT/NIV device use.

What gets measured

Vitals, symptoms, breathing pattern & respiratory status · limb ROM/oedema · mMRC · BODE · CAT · SGRQ/CRQ · pre/post-bronchodilator spirometry · 6MWT / ISWT / ESWT · FTSTS, handgrip, 1RM · MIP · functional activities & use of aids · LTOT/AOT need.

PLB

Pursed-lip breathing

↓ dynamic hyperinflation and respiratory rate; strongest evidence of all breathing exercises. Best for activity-triggered breathlessness and low peak expiratory flow.

DB

Diaphragmatic breathing

↑ parasympathetic tone, can sustain HRQoL gains for 9 months.Stop if paradoxical abdominal motion or worsening fatigue appears.

CO

Coordinated breathing + movement

Exhale on exertion/compression, inhale on rest/expansion — including breath-per-step pacing during gait.

PO

Forward-lean positioning ± rollator

↓ work of breathing and air trapping; reverses paradoxical abdominal wall motion.

FAN

Fan therapy

Cool airflow to the face stimulates trigeminal afferents; may ↓ breathlessness and ↑ exercise time.

TENS

Acupuncture / Acu-TENS / acupressure

Adjunctive; heterogeneous evidence but a recognised option for breathlessness.

MBR

Mind-body & relaxation

Tai Chi, Yoga, progressive muscle relaxation — targets the anxiety/distress component of dyspnoea.

REC

Recovery breathing sequence

Stop → recovery position → fan → prolonged exhale → relax shoulders → resume, then rest a further minute.

FET

Forced expiratory technique

Huff / cough / autogenic drainage / ACBT — first-line; active techniques preferred over passive.

C&A

Chest & abdominal compression

Only for expiratory muscle weakness — can reduce peak cough flow in COPD.

PD

Postural drainage

Adjunct when FET alone is insufficient, or for localised secretion retention.

PEP

PEP devices / acapella

Prevents airway collapse, aids patients with excessive mucus production.

P&V

Percussion & vibration

Manual technique largely ineffective; HFCWO (15–25 Hz) may help but is limited by infection-control and resource constraints.

EX

Physical exercise

Resistance training measurably improves mucus clearance; aerobic exercise alone does not.

For patients too dyspnoeic / frail for conventional training, or showing poor response to it.

NIV

Non-invasive ventilation

Rests/unloads respiratory muscles so a higher workload can be tolerated — most benefit in severe COPD / CHRF.

O₂

Supplemental oxygen during exercise

Mandatory for severe exercise-induced hypoxaemia; may aid high-intensity training even without hypoxaemia.

HFOT

High-flow oxygen therapy

↓ respiratory rate & work of breathing; may ↑ 6MWT time — evidence still emerging.

NMES

Neuromuscular electrical stimulation

For patients unable to do volitional training (acute exacerbation, profound dyspnoea, low BMI + quadriceps weakness). No dyspnoea, minimal cardio-circulatory demand.

WBV

Whole body vibration

Adjunct to resistance/squat work — improves 6MWT and FTSTS beyond resistance training alone.

ECC

Eccentric exercise / downhill walking

Greater strength gains for lower ventilatory demand — useful when dyspnoea is the limiting factor.

HYD

Hydrotherapy

Hydrostatic pressure eases expiration & air trapping; buoyancy protects arthritic joints.

LTOT

Long-term oxygen therapy

PaO₂ ≤55 mmHg / SpO₂ ≤88% (persistent), or PaO₂ 56–59 mmHg / SpO₂ 89% plus a sign of cor pulmonale (oedema, polycythaemia, pulmonary hypertension). Prescribe ≥15 h/day.

AOT

Ambulatory oxygen therapy

Offered when exercise desaturation is demonstrated and ≥2 of 3 benefit markers are met on a walk-test trial: SpO₂ ≥90% throughout · ≥10% increase in walk distance · ≥1-point Borg improvement.

Flight

In-flight oxygen

6MWT + MRC dyspnoea score screen who needs further assessment; the hypoxia challenge test (HCT) remains the clinical gold standard when indicated.

Smoking cessation

Reinforce at every session; refer to the Smoking Counselling & Cessation Centre.

Comorbidity management

Modify the exercise regimen for cardiac disease, diabetes, hypertension, arrhythmia etc.

Psychosocial support

Reinforce relaxation & dyspnoea coping skills; refer to Clinical Psychology / MSW as indicated.

Nutrition

Collaborate with dietitians on weight management to support training capacity.

Chapter 08

Infection control for face-to-face PRP

Adapted from ATS (2021) guidance for re-opening pulmonary rehabilitation programmes — layer on local HA infection-control policy on top.

Step 1

Pre-screening

  • COVID-19 screening questions
  • Provide written instructions
  • Ask patient to bring pen & water bottle
Step 2

Site preparation

  • Consult local directives
  • Adequate space for distancing
  • Hand sanitiser & disinfectant wipes
  • Masks & optimal ventilation
Step 3

During the session

  • Hand hygiene before / between / after
  • Physical distancing whenever possible
  • One pulse oximeter per patient per session
  • Sanitise equipment after each use
Step 4

After the session

  • Sanitise all surfaces & equipment
  • Repeat at end of day
