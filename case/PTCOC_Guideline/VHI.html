Ventilator Hyperinflation (VHI)

An interactive training hub summarizing clinical directives from Physiotherapy Guidelines on Ventilator Hyperinflation.

What is VHI?

Ventilator Hyperinflation (VHI) is an advanced chest physiotherapy technique mimicking manual lung expansion (MHI) directly via mechanical ventilator adjustments. Larger tidal volumes are safely delivered to clear secretions and recruit atelectatic alveoli without breaking the closed breathing circuit.

Aims of Lung Hyperinflation:

  • Secretions Mobilization: Achieved through an expiratory flow bias (Inspiratory flow < 90% of Expiratory flow rate).
  • Re-inflation of Atelectasis: Achieved by increasing airway pressure or lung volume combined with a deliberate inspiratory pause.
  • Oxygenation Support: Promotes recruitment of alveolar gas volume to maximize ventilation/perfusion (V/Q) matching.
Note: Direct comparison research shows VHI and MHI provide equivalent secretion clearance while avoiding recruitment loss.

Why VHI over MHI?

By eliminating the need to disconnect the patient from the ventilator circuit, VHI offers distinct clinical safety advantages:

Maintains PEEP Level

Prevents alveolar de-recruitment and loss of volume in PEEP-dependent patients.

Prevents Cross-Infection

Eliminates droplet/airborne dissemination during tube disconnection (vital for MDR/airborne pathogens).

Reduces VAP Incidence

Reduced circuit manipulation directly correlates to lower ventilator-associated pneumonia rates.

VHI Clinical Pathway & Appendices Guide

01

Pre-Screening

  • • Ensure personnel pass VHI competency standard (Appendix II).
  • • Review the Initial Assessment checklist.
  • • Engage ward medical officers and the nursing team in-charge.
02

Setup Checks

  • • Chart baseline vitals: HR, BP, SpO2, EtCO2, and peak/plateau pressures.
  • • Ensure Upper Pressure Limit alarm is configured to max 40cmH2O.
  • • Prior to VHI, apply suctioning using a closed system.
03

Application

  • • Adjust mechanical values stepwise per specified mode guides.
  • • Deliver in synchronized batches of 6 breaths up to a few minutes.
  • • Add manual chest shaking during expiration phases.
04

Recovery & Logs

  • • Revert ventilator values to baseline parameters immediately.
  • • Countersign document logs and confirm values with the bedside nurse.
  • • Document: achieved TV, compliance shifts, dynamic changes, and signatures.

Appendix III: Initial Assessment Checklist

Verify prior to starting any patient treatment cycle:

  • Patient's clinical state matches VHI indicators.
  • All absolute contraindications have been reviewed and ruled out.
  • Relative cautions are cleared by the Cardiopulmonary PT in-charge or senior.
  • Physician consultation completed on complicated or high-risk ICU conditions.

Appendix IV: Potential Complications & Remedial Actions

A. Hemodynamic or Respiratory Instability: If ICP exceeds parameters, HR/BP limits are violated, severe bronchospasm triggers, or pulse desaturation occurs:

➔ Immediately resume original baseline ventilator values, cease manipulation, and seek immediate ICU nursing and medical assistance.

B. Patient Agitation: If the patient is unsedated and shows intense dyssynchrony despite clinical reassurances:

➔ Stop VHI therapy immediately, reset settings, and consult ICU staff.

Mode-Specific Adjustment Matrices

Technical parameters mapping slow inhalation and expiratory flow bias (Inspiratory < 90% of Expiratory flow) as outlined in the guideline document.

Pressure-Targeted (PC)

Replicating bag squeezing slow inspiration and expiratory bias:

  • • Inspiratory Flow: Slow down flow rate by increasing rise time duration.
  • • Expiratory Flow Bias: Decrease respiratory rate (RR) and increase the I:E ratio.
  • • Hyperinflation: Stepwise increases of pressure control values, keeping PIP strict within safety levels until target TV is attained.
Volume-Targeted (VC)

Replicating bag squeezing slow inspiration and expiratory bias:

  • • Inspiratory Flow: Slow down inspiratory flow by extending duration parameters to reach peak flow.
  • • Inspiratory Hold: Manually program/increase end-inspiration pause time settings on the ventilator console.
  • • Hyperinflation: Stepwise increase in Tidal Volume (TV) with PIP closely tracked within pressure thresholds.
Spontaneous (PS/CPAP)

Replicating bag squeezing slow inspiration and expiratory bias:

  • • Cycle Threshold: Decrease the percentage of peak flow that cycles into expiration to prolong inhalation time.
  • • Inspiratory Flow: Extend flow rise duration to gently reach target pressure targets.
  • • Hyperinflation: Stepwise increases of Pressure Support with PIP strictly monitored. Consider transitioning to a VC/PC controlled mode if needed.

STRICT PHYSIOLOGIC ALARM ALERTS & LIMITS

If any parameter matches or crosses these boundaries during hyperinflation, cease VHI instantly and return ventilator settings to clinical baseline values:

< 40 cmH₂O Airway PIP
< 35 cmH₂O Plateau Pressure
< 1000 mL Max Tidal Volume
< 20 mmHg Intracranial P (ICP)
