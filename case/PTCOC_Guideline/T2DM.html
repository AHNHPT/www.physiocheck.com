Foundations

Classification of diabetes mellitus

Three clinically distinct types share the name "diabetes" — knowing which one is in front of you shapes the whole risk conversation before you even reach exercise prescription. This manual is built specifically around the first.

Type 2 (T2DM) — 90–95% of cases Type 1 (T1DM) — 5–10% of cases
90–95% of cases

Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM)

Results from insulin-resistant skeletal muscle, adipose tissue and liver, combined with a progressive insulin secretory defect. The most frequent form of diabetes among Hong Kong adults, and the focus of this manual.

5–10% of cases

Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus (T1DM)

Results from cell-mediated autoimmune destruction of the pancreatic β-cells, producing insulin deficiency. Some cases are idiopathic in origin. Not the population this manual's exercise pathway is written for.

Pregnancy-specific

Gestational Diabetes Mellitus

Screening typically occurs at 24–28 weeks of gestation, in pregnant women not previously known to have diabetes.

At a glance

What this programme is for

Weight management including dietary control and exercise education are fundamental in T2DM management. For diabetes population, every 100 minutes of physical activity per week was associated with an average reduction of 2.75mg/dl of fasting glucose and 0.14% of HbA1c according to a recent quantitative meta-analysis.

Objectives

  • Optimise glycemic control
  • Support weight management in obesity
  • Promote an active, self-managed lifestyle
  • Prevent and reduce diabetes-related complications

Target patients

  • T2DM patients on regular HA GOPC follow-up
  • Overweight patients, BMI ≥ 27.5
BMI ≥ 27.5GOPC follow-up

Stop and reconsider

  • Unstable hemodynamics / cardiac or pulmonary compromise
  • Acute MSK issue — irritable joint pain, instability, fracture, neuropathy
  • High fall risk
  • Poor cognitive function
Why it works

Four modes, four mechanisms

Each exercise mode earns its place in the prescription for a different physiological reason — combine them rather than picking one.

Aerobic

Continuous, rhythmic large-muscle movement. Improves glycemic control, insulin sensitivity, VO₂ uptake and lipid metabolism, and drives weight loss.

Every extra week of training → further HbA1c reduction

Resistance

Machines, free weights, bands or bodyweight. Builds muscle mass, strength, bone density, and improves blood pressure and lipid profile.

High intensity > low–moderate for HbA1c & insulin

HIIT

Alternating bursts of higher and lower intensity. At least equivalent benefit to moderate continuous training for HbA1c and peak oxygen uptake.

More favourable change in arterial stiffness

Balance

Core and lower-limb strength, gait training, Tai Chi. Targets the microvascular fall risk that comes with T2DM.

2.3× recurrent falls · 1.7× hip fracture risk in older T2DM

Hypoglycemia — watch for

  • Shakiness, weakness, sweating, nervousness, anxiety
  • Tingling of mouth and fingers, hunger
  • Neuroglycopenic: headache, visual disturbance, dullness, confusion, amnesia, coma

Risk is minimal in T2DM patients not on insulin/secretagogues, even with prolonged activity. Avoid exercising right before sleep — nocturnal hypoglycemia typically occurs 6–15 hours post-exercise.

Hyperglycemia / ketosis — watch for

  • Polyuria, fatigue, weakness
  • Increased thirst
  • Acetone breath

Most T2DM patients are not profoundly insulin-deficient — a high reading alone doesn't cancel the session if the patient feels well and is hydrated without ketosis. No more than 2 consecutive days without aerobic exercise, to avoid a steep drop in insulin sensitivity.

FITT reference

Exercise prescription, by mode

A stable-patient reference only — clinical judgement always overrides these defaults, and high-risk patients start lower and progress slower.

Aerobic

Type

Personal-interest, rhythmic, large-muscle activity (brisk walk + pedometer, DM aerobic dance). No more than 2 consecutive days off.

Intensity

Moderate–vigorous, guided by RPE / Talk Test / heart rate.

Duration

30–60 min/day, ≥150 min/wk moderate — or 20–60 min/day, ≥75 min/wk vigorous. Bouts of ≥10 min can be accumulated.

Frequency

Moderate ≥5 d/wk · vigorous ≥3 d/wk · or a 3–5 d/wk mix.

Progression

Shift toward vigorous intensity if fitness is the goal and not contraindicated.

Resistance

Type

Machines, free weights, bands or bodyweight. Avoid sustained static holds and the Valsalva manoeuvre.

Intensity

Moderate (≤15-rep fatigue) to vigorous (6–8-rep fatigue).

Duration

8–10 exercises, 1–3 sets × 10–15 reps to near-fatigue per set, early in training.

Frequency

≥2, preferably 3, non-consecutive days/week.

Progression

Build reps at moderate load → add load, drop to 8–10 reps once targets are consistently exceeded → add sets → then frequency.

Flexibility & Balance

Type

Static/dynamic stretching or yoga; single-leg stance, balance equipment, lower-body/core resistance, or Tai Chi.

Intensity

Stretch to tightness or mild discomfort; balance work at light–moderate intensity.

Duration

Hold 10–30 s × 2–4 reps; balance training can run any duration.

Frequency

2–3 days a week or more.

Progression

Increase duration and/or frequency over time.

Complication-specific care

Precautions by complication

T2DM rarely arrives alone — screen for these before you finalise the programme.

Retinopathy

Avoid activities that sharply raise blood pressure:
vigorous aerobic/resistance work, jumping, head-down positions, and the Valsalva manoeuvre.

Autonomic neuropathy

Blunted HR/BP response and postural hypotension risk. Monitor intensity by RPE rather than heart rate; avoid rapid postural changes.

Peripheral neuropathy & foot care

Daily foot checks to catch blisters/ulcers early. Well-fitted, shock-absorbing shoes and socks. Refer on for foot ulcer or high fall risk.

Exercise in hot weather

Skip outdoor exercise on Very Hot Weather Warning, high-humidity, no-wind days. Favour early morning/late evening, hydrate well, wear loose moisture-wicking clothing.

Contraindications & precautions — the short list

  • Unstable hemodynamic status / cardiac or pulmonary compromise
  • Irritable joint pain, joint instability, fracture, neuropathy
  • High fall risk
  • Poor cognitive function
  • Pre-exercise glucose < 3.9 mmol/L
  • Ketotic symptoms with glucose > 16.7 mmol/L and inadequate hydration
Keeping it going

Outcome measures & adherence

Only 30–40% of T2DM patients get sufficient physical activity — knowing the stage of change and the common barriers is as clinically important as the FITT prescription itself.

Stages of change

01

Precontemplation

Inactive, no intent to change in 6 months. Goal: open the conversation.

02

Contemplation

Inactive but intends to start within 6 months. Goal: build momentum.

03

Preparation

Active, but below recommended levels. Goal: reach the target dose.

04

Action

At target level for < 6 months. Goal: prevent relapse.

05

Maintenance

At target level for ≥ 6 months. Goal: sustain, vary, prevent boredom.

5 behaviour-change techniques

  • 1 Prompt focus on past success
  • 2 Identify barriers and problem-solve them together
  • 3 Use follow-up prompts
  • 4 Give concrete "where and when" instructions
  • 5 Prompt regular review of behavioural goals

Pedometer-based step goals (start feasible, build toward ~10,000/day) and internet-/app-based coaching both extend adherence beyond the clinic visit.
