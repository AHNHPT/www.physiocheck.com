Geriatric Hip Fracture Rehabilitation

The aging population demands an exponential rise in quality, standardized, and safe rehabilitation. Over 7,000 cases are admitted yearly to HA hospitals, with 80% aged ≥75. This clinical education tool is designed to train junior physiotherapists across all settings (Acute, Extended, and Community Care).

Phase 1 of 4 Pre-Operative

Acute Pre-op / Non-op

Initiate care on admission to prevent medical deterioration while optimizing the patient's condition for surgery.

Key Interventions:

  • Active deep breathing & chest PT
  • Ankle & toes circulation pumpers
  • TED stockings / IPC installation
  • Pain relief & un-involved limb training

Phase 2 of 4 Post-Operative

Acute Post-op

Targeted early mobilization starting the immediate day after surgery (unless strictly contraindicated) to minimize recumbency risk.

Key Interventions:

  • Mobilization ≥ 1x daily
  • Post-op pain control & local edema care
  • Arthroplasty precautions (Southern/Lateral)
  • Prescribe appropriate walking aid

Extended Care

Focus is restorative: maximize strength, balance, safety, and functional endurance while exploring long-term placement needs.

  • Indoor/outdoor walker & slope/stair training

Post-Discharge / NGO Support

Orthopaedic Surgical Management & Implants

Acknowledge the orthopedic approach immediately to match weight-bearing parameters and precautions.

Undisplaced Intracapsular

Stable

Commonly treated with Internal Fixation Screws to stabilize the fracture. Minimizes risk of avascular necrosis (AVN), non-union, or delayed union.

Displaced Intracapsular

Unstable / AVN Risk

Unipolar or Bipolar Hemiarthroplasty is indicated. Newer designs are preferred over Austin-Moore/Thompson due to better biomechanics and longevity.

Extracapsular Fractures

Inter / Subtrochanteric

Managed using a Sliding Hip Screw (SHS) or Intramedullary Nail (e.g., Gamma Nail for subtrochanteric/reverse oblique fractures).

Critical Proactive Monitoring Required

Post-operative geriatric hip fracture complications drive a 20-30% mortality rate in the 1st year. As a PT, you must screen every patient daily before initiating training.

1. Chest Infection Risk

Primary Signs to Screen:

Shortness of breath, localized wheezing/crackles, poor cough effort, elevated temperature, drop in SpO2.

Preventative Actions:

  • Teach active breathing & coughing pre-op
  • Postural drainage and manual chest techniques
  • Mobilize to chair early (reduces atelectasis)

2. Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)

Primary Signs to Screen:

Significant unilateral calf swelling, increased warmth, calf tenderness, and pain on passive ankle dorsiflexion.

Preventative Actions:

  • Prescribe regular active ankle pumps
  • Check lab results for safe therapeutic INR
  • Utilize sequential compression devices (SCD)

3. Hemiarthroplasty Dislocation

Primary Signs to Screen:

Significant sudden leg length discrepancy, internal rotated hip, extreme pain, or immediate loss of weight-bearing capability.

Preventative Actions:

  • Educate: No flexion >90°, adduction, internal rotation
  • Seat height adjustment (hips must be higher than knees)
  • Use an abduction wedge pillow between legs

Clinical Checklist: Patient Movement Rules (Posterior Approach Hemiarthroplasty)

Strictly Forbidden Moves

  • Sitting on low couches/sofas (knees above hips)
  • Crossing legs at the knees or ankles
  • Leaning excessively forward when sitting or standing up
  • Pivoting directly on the operated leg when turning
  • Bending down to grab objects on the floor directly

Instructed Safe Alternatives

  • Sit only in high, firm chairs (hips higher than knees)
  • Keep a firm pillow or wedge between legs in bed
  • Extend the operated leg forward during transfers
  • Use a "reacher" or adaptive "grabber" for floor items
  • Take multiple small steps instead of twisting to turn

Staging classification for basic mobility (excluding specific walking aids)

Elderly Mobility Scale (EMS)

Validated 20-point scale measuring mobility of frail older adults.

Score range: 0-20

High Dependency (<10)

Requires highest levels of manual help with ADLs and transfers. Safe home discharge unlikely without institutional or heavy care.

Borderline Care (10-13)

Requires high level of care (needs caregiver at home or nursing home support). Pivotal stage for progressive target rehabilitation.

Discharge Potential (14-20)

Generally safe for home discharge. Highly independent in basic ADLs with or without appropriate walking aid.

Scale Subcomponents:

1. Lying to Sitting • 2. Sitting to Lying • 3. Sit to Stand • 4. Standing • 5. Gait • 6. Timed Walk (6m) • 7. Functional Reach.

Timed Up and Go (TUG)

Measures the speed and functional balance for basic mobility transitions.

Timed in Seconds

Standardized Testing Procedure:

Patient starts in standard high chair, hands on thighs. On "Go", they stand, walk 3 meters, cross the line with both legs, turn toward the non-operated side, walk back, and sit. The timer stops when their back touches the chair. Record the second trial.

<10 seconds

Freely Independent

Patient is highly mobile and possesses low falls risk.

≥ 14 seconds

High Fall Risk

Strongly correlates with increased incidence of post-fracture community falls.

Clinical Reasoning Quiz

1
Acute Post-op Clinical Reasoning

An 82-year-old female is Day 1 post-operative following a right posterior approach bipolar hemiarthroplasty. Her baseline vital signs are stable, and resting pain is rated 3/10 on NPRS. How should the junior therapist proceed with early mobilization?

Correct Answer: B

NICE Guidelines (CG124) and HA PT guidelines recommend starting mobilization (bedside transfer, standing, walking) at least once daily starting the day after surgery, unless strictly medically contraindicated. Waiting is unnecessary and increases the risk of hypostatic pneumonia, DVT, and deconditioning.

2
Complication Safety Screening

While performing post-op evaluation on Day 3, you note the patient's operated calf is warm to touch, swollen (+2.5 cm circumference compared to contralateral leg), and painful. They deny chest pain or shortness of breath. Which action is strictly indicated?

Correct Answer: C

Asymmetry, redness, and calf swelling indicate suspected Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT). Massages, physical therapy, or active movement are contraindicated to prevent dislodging the thrombus, which could lead to a fatal pulmonary embolism. Report to medical staff immediately for diagnostic screening (Doppler/D-dimer).

Outcome Interpretation 3

Prior to planned discharge from Extended Care, an 80-year-old patient achieves an Elderly Mobility Scale (EMS) score of 12. According to guidelines, how should this score dictate discharge planning?

Correct Answer: B

Smith (1994) clinical interpretation guidelines state that EMS scores between 10-13 indicate high care needs, requiring either a caregiver or residential home assistance. Safe independent discharge home requires a score between 14-20.

4
Hemiarthroplasty Protection

You are advising a caregiver on home modifications and positioning rules for a patient who had a southern approach hemiarthroplasty. Which layout recommendation violates post-op safety guidelines?

Correct Answer: B

Posterior and posterolateral approaches (sometimes referred to as southern) disrupt posterior structures, making joint dislocation high-risk during hip flexion >90°, internal rotation, or adduction. Low, soft couches force deep hip flexion (>90°) and are strictly contraindicated.

5
Sarcopenia Integration

A 74-year-old female patient with an intertrochanteric fracture (repaired with SHS) is tested for Handgrip Strength on Day 5 post-op. She scores a maximum of 14.2 kg. According to AWGS 2019 standards, how should this score guide you?

Correct Answer: B

The Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia (AWGS 2019) cutoff for low handgrip strength is <18.0 kg for women (<28.0 kg for men). Low grip strength correlates with delayed ambulation, poor functional independence, and higher early mortality. Therapists should plan progressive, structured nutritional and resistance training.
