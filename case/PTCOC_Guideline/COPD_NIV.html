01 Clinical Indications & Contraindications for Acute NIV

Ref: BTS/ICS & GOLD Guideline Standards

Clinical Indications (Start Criteria)

Initiate if respiratory failure from acute COPD exacerbation persists despite optimal medical therapy:

  • Hypercapnic Respiratory Acidosis (Arterial pH < 7.35, PaCO2 > 6.5 kPa)
  • Respiratory Rate (RR) > 23 breaths per minute
  • Gold Standard Target Zone: Highly effective if pH is titrated between 7.25 and 7.35.
  • NIV may still assist if pH is 7.15 - 7.25, but failure rate increases. Escalate monitoring.

Absolute Contraindications (No-Go Criteria)

  • Severe facial deformity or facial burns / trauma / surgical sites
  • Fixed upper airway obstruction
  • Cardiorespiratory arrest / inability to maintain spontaneous effort
  • Haemodynamic instability (hypotension, serious cardiac dysrhythmias)
  • Inability to protect airway or clear copious, thick secretions
  • Untreated pneumothorax
  • Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) rhinorrhoea

Relative Contraindications & Vigilance Checklist

Presence of any of these indicators warrants immediate consideration for placement in high dependency (HDU) or intensive care units (ICU), and rapid planning for invasive mechanical ventilation if NIV failure occurs:

Infection Control Plan under CCIDER (2024) Guidelines

Aerosol-Generating Procedures (AGP)

NIV (BiPAP & CPAP), open suctioning, sputum induction, awake bronchoscopy, endotracheal intubation/extubation, and CPR are documented high-risk AGPs. Nebulizer therapy, NPS, NPA, and high-flow oxygen (≥ 6 L/min) are theoretically at risk of droplet dispersal and should be performed inside high-risk areas.

02 Interface Selection, Exhalation Valves & Mask Fitting

Full-Face Mask (Oro-Nasal Interface)

Default Acute Choice
PROS & INDICATIONS
  • Ideal default starting interface in acute hypercapnic respiratory failure.
  • Effectively mitigates severe open-mouth air leaks to maintain set target IPAP/EPAP.
  • Perfect for edentulous patients with high clinical distress profiles.
CONS & CLINICAL RISKS
  • Obstructs expectoration, oral communication, eating, and drinking.
  • Elevated hazard of gastric aspiration if vomiting or hematemesis occurs.
  • Increased rebreathing volume and potential for claustrophobic patient distress.
Mask Rotation Strategy (Brill, 2014)

During the decompensated phase, patients often require 24-hour continuous NIV. To avoid skin breakdown, **rotate interfaces**: utilize an oro-nasal mask during daytime hours and switch to a total face mask during sleep. This alternates contact zones, allowing localized tissue recovery and regular pressure relief for the vulnerable nasal bridge.

Exhalation Port Guide & Safety Restrictions

Single limb circuits must have open exhalation ports to vent exhaled CO2. Never tape or occlude ports.

1. Built-in Exhalation Port

Built within the mask. Avoid in high-risk infectious cohorts to prevent diffuse aerosol contamination.

2. Disposable Port

Attached circuit element for non-vented masks. Must always match a bacterial filter upstream.

3. Plateau Expiratory Valve

Features variable diameter apertures to maintain constant flow across operating pressures.

4. Whisper Swivel II Valve

Directs exhaled gas quietly. Swivels freely with the CPAP tubing to eliminate kinks and maintain consistent flow.

Humidification Strategy (BTS/ICS Standards)

Humidification is **not** routinely indicated in hypercapnic respiratory failure. However, heated wire or heated pass-over humidification must be configured immediately if the patient reports significant mucosal/oral dryness, or if respiratory secretions become thick and tenacious, to avoid sputum impaction and dynamic circuit resistance.

04 Patient-Ventilator Asynchrony Debugger

Identify Patient Response Mismatch:

Airway Flow / Pressure Waveform Simulation Baseline Expiratory Plateau (Offset by EPAP)

Ineffective Triggering

The patient initiates a spontaneous effort, but the ventilator fails to trigger IPAP flow. This causes sudden drops in circuit pressure and massive muscle strain.

CLINICAL MECHANISMS
  • High intrinsic PEEP (PEEPi) from gas trapping.
  • Trigger sensitivity configured too insensitive.
  • High circuit leaks preventing pressure/flow drop detection.
PHYSIOTHERAPEUTIC REMEDIES
  • Titrate EPAP upward to match and offset PEEPi.
  • Increase trigger sensitivity settings.
  • Resolve mask leaks using stability adjusters.

05 Airway Secretions & Open Suction Sequence Protocol

1. Active Clearance Techniques

During planned NIV breaks, teach **Active Cycle of Breathing Techniques (ACBT)**, relaxed controlled breathing, pursed-lips breathing, and **huffing** to mobilize secretions from peripheral airways without collapsing vulnerable bronchiole walls.

2. Mechanical Oscillatory Aids

Integrate **OPEP (Oscillating Positive Expiratory Pressure)** devices (e.g., Acapella or Flutter) or manual chest wall maneuvers (percussion, vibration) during scheduled NIV downtime. Ensure baseline SpO2 remains above 90%.

3. Tracheostomy & Neuromuscular Care

For cohorts with comorbid neuromuscular weakness, consider **Mechanical In-Exsufflation (MIE)** to generate high simulated cough flows. In patients unable to clear sputum, execute open suctioning following the safe sequence below.

Mandatory Open Suctioning Protocol Sequence

Patients on NIV have high baseline pressure requirements. Disconnecting the interface causes rapid alveolar derecruitment, hypoxia, and aerosol generation. Strictly execute the following sequence:

1

Patient Prep & Pre-Oxygenation

Confirm patient's consent. Increase FiO2 on the ventilator to pre-oxygenate for 1-2 minutes. Ensure baseline SpO2 remains stable above 92%.

2

Set NIV to Stand-by & Remove Interface

Place the ventilator on Stand-by mode (do not leave on active flow to prevent massive aerosol dispersal). Quickly unlock and slide the mask off the patient.

3

Immediate Oxygen Delivery Device (ODD) Swap

To avoid rapid hypoxia, immediately apply a matching ODD with comparable FiO2:

  • For FiO2 0.21 - 0.40: Nasal Cannula at 1-6 L/min
  • For FiO2 0.40 - 0.60: Simple Oxygen Mask at 5-8 L/min
  • For FiO2 > 0.60: Non-rebreathing mask at 12-15 L/min or HFNO
4

Aseptic Tracheal/Oral Suctioning

Perform open suctioning following strict infection control guidelines. Keep catheter passes under **15 seconds**. Continuously monitor ECG heart rate and rhythm.

5

Re-apply Mask & Restart Ventilator Flow

Remove the temporary ODD, quickly reposition and secure the mask, adjust strap tension (confirming the 1-2 finger standard), press **Restart** to resume active pressure, and coach the patient to synchronize.

06 NIV Weaning Protocols & Rehabilitation Framework

Criteria for Initiating NIV Weaning (Faverio et al., 2019)

Only attempt weaning if the patient's acute pathology is resolving and they meet all of the following physiological criteria:

Arterial pH ≥ 7.35
SpO2 > 90% on FiO2 ≤ 50% (PaO2/FiO2 > 200)
Respiratory Rate (RR) ≤ 25 bpm
Heart Rate (HR) ≤ 120 bpm & SBP ≥ 90 mmHg
No active respiratory distress / agitation
Patient alert, cooperative & GCS ≥ 8
Critical Action Note: If any signs of severe dyspnoea or arterial CO2 spikes appear during weaning, immediately resume the previous stable parameters and delay further tapering.

Physiotherapy Clinical Rehabilitation Framework

Phase A: Acute / Decompensated

Decompensated gases (pH < 7.35)

  • Goal: Reduce WOB, optimize V/Q match, clearance.
  • Breathing control training & ventilator synchrony.
  • Bedside ROM and ankle pumps to prevent deep vein thrombosis.
  • Targeted NMES (Neuromuscular Electrical Stimulation) if indicated.
Phase B: Compensated

Compensated gases (pH ≥ 7.35)

  • Goal: Facilitate weaning, physical mobilization.
  • Bedside active range of motion & active ankle pumps.
  • Graduated sitting out on the chair.
  • Supported bedside standing & stepping exercises.
Phase C: Pre-Discharge

Stable gases / Outpatient prep

  • Goal: Ambulation, muscle endurance, home care.
  • Ambulation exercises (with portable O2 or NIV).
  • Home safety checks & caregiver device education.
  • Referral to outpatient cardiorespiratory training.

CASE 1

An acute COPD patient in the Medical Ward presents with a PaCO2 of 8.2 kPa, pH of 7.28, and a respiratory rate of 28 bpm. They exhibit a wet, weak cough with thick secretions and are undergoing continuous BiPAP treatment. What is the correct sequence of clinical actions for secretion clearance?

Rationale: Slide-under suctioning is strictly prohibited under CCIDER 2024 (creates severe aerosol dispersal and breaks pressure seals). Leaving the patient on room air risks immediate desaturation. Putting the ventilator on "Stand-by", providing a matching ODD (Oxygen Delivery Device) during the procedure, performing rapid aseptic suction, and reapplying the interface with optimal tension is the correct protocol (page 42-43).

CASE 2

During bedside assessment, a junior physiotherapist notices a patient on BiPAP (IPAP 16, EPAP 4) showing accessory muscle recruitment and nasal flaring. Looking at the screen, a transient airway pressure drop is observed during patient's spontaneous effort without the machine triggering IPAP. What is the priority troubleshooting step?

Rationale: The clinical signs indicate Ineffective Triggering due to intrinsic PEEP (PEEPi). Increasing EPAP helps match and neutralize the patient's intrinsic expiratory pressure barrier, allowing their spontaneous effort to trigger the flow valve. Adjusting trigger sensitivity is also a useful secondary approach (page 34-35).

CASE 3

You are planning the rehabilitation program for a 68-year-old patient with an acute exacerbation of COPD. Their arterial blood gas results are decompensated (pH of 7.31, PaCO2 of 7.6 kPa, and SpO2 of 89%). According to the rehabilitation framework, what is the most appropriate physical therapy intervention?

Rationale: During the **acute phase with decompensated blood gases (pH < 7.35)**, metabolic demand must be minimized. Progressive mobilizations, stepping, or resistive exercise can worsen acidosis. Only passive/active-assisted ROM and ankle pumps (to prevent deep vein thrombosis) and NMES are indicated. Bedside standing is reserved for the compensated phase (pH ≥ 7.35) (page 5, 43, 46).

CASE 4

During telemedicine surveillance, you retrieve telemetry data for a patient using home NIV. It shows a Total Study Time (TST) of only 2.5 hours/night, high leak levels (48 L/min average), and target tidal volume dropping to 3.6 ml/kg. The patient reports waking with oral dryness and chest tightness. What is the priority management strategy?

Rationale: The high leakage rate (48 L/min exceeds the 7-25 L/min threshold) is causing loss of tidal volume support, waking dryness, and poor compliance. Increasing pressure would exacerbate the leak. Correcting the leak interface and initiating heated humidification is the first-line solution (page 17, 31, 48-49).

CASE 5

You are tracking overnight pulse oximetry data for a home NIV patient. The telemonitoring report displays persistent desaturation dips below 90% lasting for 8 consecutive minutes, reaching a lowest nadir of 83%. According to the newly expanded HA V4 guidelines, how is this oximetry profile defined, and what is your immediate clinical course?

Rationale: Section 2.4.3.2 (Telemonitoring of pulse oximetry) explicitly defines an abnormal oximetry pattern in COPD as a saturation below 90% for 5 minutes or longer, reaching a nadir of 85% or lower (or ≥ 30% of total recording time spent under 90%). Early assessment of compliance alongside clinician consultation manages underlying condition progression before an emergency hospital readmission becomes necessary (page 49).
