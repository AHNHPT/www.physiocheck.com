Guideline Objectives & Background

Objectives

  • Facilitate safe clinical practices when providing physical exercise training to patients with bone metastases.
  • Assist physiotherapists in designing individualized, evidence-based, structured rehabilitation plans.
  • Proactively minimize potential risks of skeletal-related incidents, pathological fracture, or neurological deterioration.

The Clinical Challenge

Prescribing exercise therapy or delivering ambulation training to patients with bone metastases is highly challenging.

While clinical evidence demonstrates that physiotherapy has a considerable role in counteracting the adverse effects of prolonged bed rest (atrophy, functional decline, fatigue), concerns regarding pathological fracture or spinal cord compression often lead to over-restrictive practice. Secure clinical practice relies on modern risk stratification models.

Phase 1

Before Exercise

  • ✓ Review medical history, imaging findings, and diagnostic stability status.
  • ✓ Use the official Checklist for Mobilization to screen for mechanical risk.
  • ✓ Ensure patient/caregivers understand risks and benefits of active rehabilitation vs. absolute bed rest.
  • ✓ Prepare orthoses, walking aids, and mechanical handling tools (hoist/draw sheets).
  • ✓ Secure adequate clinical manpower for transfers and gait phases.
Phase 2

During Exercise

  • ✓ Provide comfortable care and prescribe exercises strictly in pain-free active range of motion.
  • ✓ Closely monitor condition before, during, and after movements to detect signs of fresh fracture or pain.
  • ⚠️ AVOID: Manual muscle testing (MMT), structural weight bearing, resistance training, stretching, or twisting of involved bone segments.
  • ✓ Stop immediately if the patient reports any sudden, drastic increase in physical pain.
  • ✓ Pace tasks carefully to prevent over-fatigue.
Phase 3

After Exercise

  • ✓ Monitor for delayed-onset neurological signs or spinal column changes.
  • 🚨 Red Flag Actions: Immediately notify ward medical officers and nurse in-charge if there is:
  • Sudden drop in local sensory integrity
    New lancinating / radiating pain
    Sudden-onset weakness (Myotomal)
    Signs of spinal cord, nerve root, or cauda equina compression
  • ✓ Document all specific tolerances, assistive setups, and mobility milestones thoroughly in the physiotherapy progress notes.

Instability Risk Factors Info

1. Anatomical Site: Cervical and lumbar columns lack structural reinforcement and carry larger instability risks than thoracic vertebrae protected by the chest wall/ribcage.

2. Tumor Extent: Collapse or progressive infiltration of vertebral body structures weakens structural capacity.

3. Co-morbidities: Age-related osteoporosis or long-term systemic steroid therapy exacerbates bone failure risk.

Frankel Neurological Classification

Grades neurological injury severity and sensory/motor sparing levels below spine lesions.

Grade A (Paraplegia): No sensation, complete motor paralysis.
Grade B (Sensory Only): Some sensation remains; complete motor paralysis.
Grade C (Non-Ambulatory): Non-functional, unserviceable motor sparing.
Grade D (Ambulatory): Sparing of serviceable motor function; patients can walk.
Grade E (Normal): Full motor and sensory preservation with zero deficits.

Harrington Pathological Classifications

Focuses on spinal structural collapse and corresponding sensory-motor impairment.

Class 1: No neurological signs.
Class 2: Bone involvement without collapse or instability.
Class 3: Significant neurological impairment without spinal bone collapse.
Class 4: Vertebral collapse with pain / instability but zero neurological impairment.
Class 5: Vertebral collapse with pain / instability paired with neurological loss.

ASIA Impairment Scale (Impairment)

Neurological assessment standard grading complete/incomplete spinal cord injury statuses.

A (Complete): No motor/sensory function preserved in sacral segments S4-S5.
B (Incomplete): Sensory, but not motor function is preserved below neurologic level (includes S4-S5).
C (Incomplete): Motor preserved below neurological level, >50% of key muscles have grade < 3.
D (Incomplete): Motor preserved below neurological level, ≥50% of key muscles have grade ≥ 3.
E (Normal): Normal motor and sensory statuses throughout.
