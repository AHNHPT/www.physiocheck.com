🧪 Key Points

  • This is a case admitted to orthopedic ward with a one-year history of low back pain that has worsened over the past month, currently causing him unable to walk. The patient has a history of colon cancer with lung metastasis. Severe back pain with history of cancer raises a major red flag for spinal metastasis or pathological fracture.
  • Recognizing Red Flags for Spinal Metastasis: A history of cancer — such as radiological colon cancer with lung metastases — is a major red flag when evaluating low back pain. This history, combined with severe pain that prevents walking and specific L5 midline tenderness, should immediately elevate clinical suspicion for bony metastasis and potential pathological fractures.
  • Interpreting Elevated Inflammatory Markers: An ESR of 90 is a significantly elevated result. In the context of the patient's oncology history, this laboratory finding strongly supports the need to rule out sinister pathologies like metastasis or infection before treating the pain as a standard mechanical issue.
  • Contraindications and Precaution in Physical Therapy — Manual Therapy: Applying direct mechanical force, such as a Central PA mobilization on L5, is strictly contraindicated due to the high risk of causing or exacerbating a fracture in a structurally compromised spine.
  • Clinical Judgment vs. Medical Prescriptions: Even when a medical management plan includes orders like "PT x FWB walking exercise", the physiotherapist would access the patient's physical ability and radiological findings. Raising discussion on need for orthosis with the team would be a good idea in this case.
