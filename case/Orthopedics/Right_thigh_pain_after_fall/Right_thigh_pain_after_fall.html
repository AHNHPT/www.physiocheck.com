🧪 Learning Points

This case is a 70-year-old male admitted to orthopedic ward with increasing right thigh pain following a recent fall onto his buttock and right leg. It is important to take note that he has a past history of prostate cancer treated with radiation therapy in 2020. Although he was initially able to walk with a stick immediately after the fall, his pain has worsened, and a right medial thigh bruise is now present. The medical team has ordered X-rays of his pelvis, right hip, right femur, which is currently pending, alongside a referral for physiotherapy mobilization.

  • Prioritizing Radiological Clearance: A history of prostate cancer combined with a recent traumatic fall places this patient at high risk for pathological fractures. Pending X-rays of the pelvis, hip, and femur must be reviewed and cleared before any joint loading occurs.
  • Independent Clinical Judgment: Even though the medical management plan explicitly requests "Physiotherapy x Mobilization," the physiotherapist must act as a safety gatekeeper. Full weight-bearing walking and resisted bed exercises must be strictly withheld until structural integrity is confirmed.
  • Avoiding Provocative Testing: Diagnostic maneuvers that apply compression or shear forces to the joint, such as the Hip Quadrant test, are completely contraindicated when an acute fracture has not yet been ruled out.
  • Safe Acute Management: While the patient is on bed rest awaiting imaging, physiotherapy interventions should be restricted to safe, distal, unloaded movements—such as ankle pumps—to promote circulation and prevent deep vein thrombosis.
