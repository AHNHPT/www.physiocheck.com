This is a bedside simulation, not a page of text. On a phone screen it will still run, but it becomes much harder to use:

  • The patient monitor and ventilator stack below the scene instead of sitting beside it, so you cannot watch him and the numbers at the same time.
  • The mobilisation ladder and the room lose most of their detail.
  • The deteriorations give you thirty seconds to choose — scrolling a phone costs you several of them.

A tablet in landscape is fine. A laptop is better.

PneumoSim 02 · ICU Physiotherapy

Getting him out of bed.Day 8. Ventilated. Isolated inhalation injury, no skin burns. Eight days flat on his back and now he cannot lift his arm off the sheet. Your job this morning is to sit him on the edge of the bed, stand him, and put him back — without losing the airway.

ICU · Bed 4 Day 8 · PSV MRC 42/60 ICU Mobility Scale 1 ~30–40 min

You will take the handover, screen him against the safety criteria, examine him, set a target, build the team, and then mobilise him in real time on a live monitor. He may deteriorate and you will have seconds, not minutes, to decide.

Everything you choose is logged and marked. At the end you get a debrief you can print.

Mr. Leung is a fictional patient. The scenario is built around a major residential high-rise fire but is not an account of any real person or any real incident, and the clinical details have been written for teaching rather than drawn from a case record.

Best on a laptop or tablet in landscape. Headphones recommended — the ventilator, the alarms and his breathing are all synthesised live.

This is AI generated teaching simulation. The patient, results and images are fabricated for education and must not be used for clinical decision-making. Follow your own unit's protocols.
