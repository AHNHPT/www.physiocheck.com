🧪 Investigations – Explanation

  • Check Hstix: Correct, low blood sugar (hypoglycemia) can cause shortness of breath (SOB) because the body releases stress hormones like adrenaline, which can lead to rapid breathing, and because the respiratory muscles need glucose to function properly. Hypoglycemia can also trigger anxiety, causing hyperventilation, which also leads to a feeling of breathlessness.
    In this case, spot Hstix was 7.8mmol/L, which is in normal range. This rule out hypoglycemia causing breathlessness.
  • Check pupil size and reflex: Incorrect. Pupil asymmetry or pupil dilation may be a sign of a serious neurological condition, such as optic nerve/brain damage. However, the checking of pupil is less improtant in this case as patient was fully awake, lack of history of neurological demage and no other associated neurological symptoms/sign.

  • Ascultation of lungs: Correct, Patient presented with excertional SOB, boderline SpO2. Cardio-respiratory dysfunction was the 1st consideration. Dull lung sound and decreased vocal resonance suggests a lack of normal lung tissue or the presence of fluid or air between the lung and chest wall, as seen in a pleural effusion or pneumothorax. With patient history of untreated cancer, pleural effusion was highly susspected clinically.

    Malignant pleural effusion most often occurs with advanced stage 4 cancer, including lung and breast cancer. It is usually a sign of a poorer outcome and shortened life expectancy.


    img: Complete white out the Rt pleural space indicating severe pleural effusion and lost of Rt lung volume.

  • Check subconjunctiva circulation: Correct. Conjunctival pallor is present if there is loss of the normal redness of the anterior rim of the conjunctiva, which is highly suggestive of anemia.(LR 4.49, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.80, 10.99)(1) Severe anemia can presented with tiredness and SOB. If any suspection of acute anemia, futher questioning of other causes maybe indicated, for example GI bleed or recent trauma.

💊 Management – Explanation

  • Mobilise patient: Incorrect. Based on the clinical findings, pleural effusion is highly suspected. Pneumothorax is considered unlikely, as it is typically associated with decreased/absent breath sounds and hyperresonance on percussion secondary to trapped air, which were not observed in this case. Given the borderline SpO₂ even at rest, the pleural effusion is most likely moderate to severe. Position changes or turning may exacerbate the condition and lead to further desaturation. Therefore, mobilisation of the patient should be approached with caution, as it may cause more harm than benefit.

  • Refer GOPC same day: Incorrect. Based on the clinical findings, a large/symptomatic pleural effusion is highly suspected and constitutes a medical emergency requiring prompt assessment and intervention.

  • Call ambulance Correct. It is recommended to remain with the patient until the ambulance staff arrive. Continuously monitor the patient’s condition and vital signs. Where possible, attempt to stabilise the patient’s condition.

  • Position patient in Right side lying: Incorrect. According to the ascultation, Right pleural effusion was highly suspected. Position patient with good lung lying below can enhance better V/Q ratio, thus improves satuation. Position paient in LEFT slide lying would be a better option.
